How the savings are estimated
  • Brands match generics with the same RxNorm clinical drug (ingredient, strength and dose form) and an FDA "A" therapeutic-equivalence rating; authorized generics always match. Reference biologics match biosimilars with the same concentration, volume and device.
  • The alternative's price is the volume-weighted average Medicaid paid per unit for the matched products in the same state (national when a state has too little volume).
  • Minimum rebates use the statutory floor (23.1% of price for brands, 17.1% for clotting factors and pediatric drugs, 13% for generics). Average rebates use 61.9% for brands, calibrated to MACPAC's FY 2024 totals ($58.4B rebates on $106.4B gross); authorized generics and biosimilars stay at 23.1%. Rebates are applied to Medicaid paid amounts, and confidential state supplemental rebates are not included.
  • Brand-preferred lists are compiled for SC, IA, MD, NH, OH and NY (2026 lists). Other states may have lists that are not yet included.
  • Interchangeable biosimilars can be substituted by the pharmacy where state law allows. Other biosimilars need a new prescription.

How alternatives and reasons are found
  • An alternative has the same ingredient, strength and dose form (RxNorm clinical drug) and the same billing unit. Generics must carry an FDA "A" therapeutic-equivalence rating; authorized generics always qualify. Biosimilars must match concentration, volume and device.
  • Prices are national averages of what Medicaid paid per unit in 2025, before rebates. Savings estimates come from the Savings Opportunity Overview.
  • When no alternative was dispensed, the reason comes from the FDA Orange Book (generic approvals, listed patents, exclusivities) or the Purple Book (licensed biosimilars, exclusivities). Listed patents can be challenged, settled or ruled invalid, so their dates are not guaranteed launch dates.

How to read these numbers
  • Spending is before manufacturer rebates, so it overstates Medicaid's net cost, especially for brand drugs.
  • Reimbursement includes pharmacy dispensing fees.
  • “Generic competition” means a generic of the same strength and form was dispensed to Medicaid in 2025 (national data). Manufacturers’ own CMS categories often disagree, so they are not used. A brand can appear in both brand categories when only some strengths have generics.
  • Prescription counts are not adjusted for days supply: a 90-day fill counts the same as a 30-day fill.
  • Source: CMS State Drug Utilization Data 2025 (national totals), FDA NDC Directory, Purple Book and Medicaid Drug Rebate Program product data; ingredients from NLM RxNorm.
